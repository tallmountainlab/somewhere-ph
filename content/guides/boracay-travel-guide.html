For many Filipino families, Boracay is where the summer photos were taken: a sunburned cousin holding up a mango shake, a wedding party barefoot in the sand, a teenager squinting into the sunset with a white sail behind. The sand is the thing everyone remembers, so fine and powdery that it squeaks under your feet and stays cool even at noon. Generations later, people still come for exactly that, and it is still as soft as the old photos promised.

Boracay is a small island, only about seven kilometers long, off the northwest tip of Panay in the Western Visayas. It is one of the most famous beach destinations in Asia, and also one of the easiest to reach in the Philippines.

Getting there

Most visitors fly from Manila or Cebu to one of two airports on Panay:

  • Caticlan (Godofredo P. Ramos Airport): the closest. From the airport, it is a short ride to the Caticlan jetty port and a boat crossing of around 15 minutes.
  • Kalibo: bigger, with more flights, including some international ones, but about 1.5 to 2 hours by road to the jetty port.

Expect to pay a terminal fee, an environmental fee and a boat fare at the port. On the island, tricycles and e-trikes take you to your hotel.

White Beach and the three stations

The heart of the island is White Beach, around four kilometers of sand on the west coast. It is divided into three areas named after the old boat stations where ferries once landed:

  • Station 1: the widest, quietest-feeling stretch of sand, with more upscale resorts and the little grotto at Willy's Rock.
  • Station 2: the lively middle, with D'Mall, restaurants, bars and the most action at night.
  • Station 3: more relaxed and budget-friendly, with smaller resorts.

Best things to do

  • Sunset on a paraw: these traditional double-outrigger sailboats glide along White Beach in the late afternoon. It is the classic Boracay moment.
  • Puka Shell Beach: a quieter, wilder beach on the north end, named for the small shells once collected there.
  • Island hopping: boat trips to snorkeling spots and nearby islets such as Crystal Cove and Crocodile Island.
  • Mount Luho: the island's highest point, with a lookout over both coasts.
  • Bulabog Beach: on the east side, a hub for kitesurfing and windsurfing in the windy months.

When to go

The best weather is usually from about November to May, during the amihan season, when White Beach is calm and sunny. From about June to October, the southwest monsoon, or habagat, brings rain, waves on White Beach and the chance of typhoons, and boats may use a different port on the other side of the island. Holy Week, Christmas and New Year are the busiest times, so book early.

Island rules since the closure

In 2018, the government closed Boracay to tourists for six months to clean up the island after years of overdevelopment and sewage problems. It reopened with new rules, and many are still in place:

  • Only stay in accommodations accredited by the Department of Tourism. You may be asked for a hotel booking confirmation at the port.
  • Drinking alcohol and smoking on the beach are not allowed, and neither are parties on the sand.
  • Do not take sand or shells, and avoid single-use plastic.

Rules and fees can change, so check the latest before you travel.

Then and now

Not so long ago, Boracay was an island of fishing families and coconut farms. Early travelers in the 1970s and 1980s slept in simple nipa huts, ate grilled fish by lamplight and walked the whole beach without seeing a building taller than a palm tree. By the 1990s, the secret was out, and the island grew fast, sometimes too fast. The 2018 closure was a hard pause for the families who depend on tourism, but it gave the island a chance to breathe. Today, wider beach easements and cleaner water mean the view at sunset looks a lot more like those first faded photos: soft white sand, a row of sails on the water and the sky turning orange behind them.

Practical tips

  • Money: there are ATMs on the island, but lines can be long, so carry some cash.
  • Getting around: walking along the beach path is often the easiest way between stations.
  • Sun: the reflection off white sand is strong, so use reef-safe sunscreen and a hat.
