El Nido and Coron, Palawan: how to plan the trip

How to get to El Nido and Coron, which island-hopping tours to book, and how to travel between them.

There was a time when El Nido was a sleepy fishing town at the end of a long, bumpy road, a place backpackers whispered about and almost nobody else could find. Electricity came on in the evening, the town went quiet early, and the only sound at night was the sea. The roads are paved now and the secret is out, but stand on the beach as the bangkas head out at sunrise and you will still feel what those first travelers felt: that you have found the edge of the world.

El Nido

El Nido sits on Bacuit Bay, whose islands and lagoons are reached by day-trip boat. Tours are sold as fixed routes, commonly labeled A, B, C and D, and visitors pay a small environmental fee that is valid for several days.

  • Tour A is the classic first-day pick, with lagoons and beaches close to town.
  • Tour C visits hidden beaches and is often rated the most scenic.
  • Nacpan Beach, about 30 to 45 minutes north by road, is a long, quiet beach worth a half day.

Some lagoons limit visitor numbers and require a booked time slot or kayak, so book these through your tour operator a day or two ahead in high season.

Getting to El Nido

You can fly directly into El Nido's small airport from Manila (fares are higher, but it saves a long transfer), or fly into Puerto Princesa and take a van or bus north, which takes roughly five to six hours.

Coron

Coron is famous for Kayangan Lake, Twin Lagoon and some of the best wreck diving in the world, from Japanese ships sunk in 1944. Even non-divers can snorkel over shallow wrecks such as the Skeleton Wreck. Mount Tapyas, a short climb from town, gives a good sunset view.

Fly into Busuanga airport, about 30 to 40 minutes by van from Coron town.

El Nido to Coron by boat

Fast ferries link El Nido and Coron in about four hours, weather permitting. In rough seas, sailings can be cancelled, so keep your schedule flexible. Multi-day expedition boat trips that camp on islands along the way are another option if you have three to five days and do not mind basic conditions.

Practical tips

  • Bring cash. Card acceptance is limited and ATMs can run out.
  • Pack a dry bag and water shoes for boat days.
  • Electricity and internet are much better than a decade ago but can still drop out.

Memory laneThen and now

Older visitors remember the trip north from Puerto Princesa as an all-day adventure in a jeepney with chickens under the seats, and a town where generators hummed after dark. In Coron, the Second World War wrecks have rested on the seabed since 1944. Divers still swim past their hulls in water so clear it feels like looking through old window glass. The comforts have arrived, but the lagoons look exactly the way they did in the faded postcards.
