For generations of Filipino families, a trip to Cebu meant lechon, a whole roasted pig with skin like glass, and a stop at Magellan's Cross. A trip to Bohol meant a school photo in front of the Chocolate Hills, everyone squinting into the sun. If you have one of those photos in an old album somewhere, this is your sign to go back. If you don't, this is your chance to take one.

South Cebu

  • Moalboal is known for its huge sardine run just off Panagsama Beach, plus a good chance of seeing sea turtles while snorkeling.
  • Kawasan Falls in Badian has bright blue pools. Canyoneering tours jump, slide and swim their way down the river to the falls.
  • Expect three to four hours by road from Cebu City to Moalboal.
About whale shark feeding in Oslob: many conservation groups advise against it because the sharks are hand-fed to keep them in one place. If you want to see whale sharks, look for operators that follow responsible, non-feeding guidelines.

Bohol

  • The Chocolate Hills, more than a thousand grassy domes that turn brown in the dry season.
  • Tarsiers, tiny primates best seen at the Philippine Tarsier Foundation sanctuary in Corella, where visits are quiet and no flash photography is allowed.
  • The Loboc River, for a slow riverboat lunch cruise.
  • Panglao Island, connected to Bohol by bridge, for beaches, diving and day trips to Balicasag Island.

Getting between Cebu and Bohol

Fast ferries run several times a day between Cebu City and Tagbilaran, Bohol, taking about two hours. Bohol-Panglao International Airport also has direct flights from Manila and some international routes.

Sample five-day plan

  1. Arrive in Cebu, transfer to Moalboal.
  2. Sardine run snorkel in the morning, Kawasan Falls canyoneering in the afternoon.
  3. Return to Cebu City and ferry to Bohol.
  4. Countryside day: Chocolate Hills, tarsier sanctuary, Loboc River.
  5. Beach and snorkel day on Panglao, then fly or ferry out.

Memory laneThen and now

Bohol has long been the destination of class field trips and family reunions. Many people remember the Loboc River lunch cruise with a local band playing old love songs, and that tradition carries on today. The 2013 earthquake damaged some of Bohol's centuries-old churches, and their careful restoration has been a labor of love. Visiting them is a way to see how much the island cares about its past.
